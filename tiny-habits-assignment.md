## Tiny Habits Assignment

#### Question 1: In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

"Relying primarily on motivation (and willpower) to change your behaviour long term is a losing strategy".
The easiest way long term behavioural change can be achieved is by creating tiny habits. Habits that can be replicated every day. "Plant a tiny seed in the right spot and it will grow without coaxing". We need to look at our lives, look at what we want to change and break it down to tiny behaviours and put them in the right spot and allow them to grow. And one fine day, when we look back, we will have achieved magnificent change and realise that we did not need motivation or willpower, we only needed little habits that we could do daily without forcing ourselves to.

#### Question 2: How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P.

B=MAP
BJ Fog says that in order to create a tiny behaviours (B), which you have the ability (A) to do easily and require little to no motivation (M) to sustain, you only need one tiny tigger or prompt (P) to execute that behaviour and turn it into a habit without even thinking about it. He adds that you must use your existing behaviour as a trigger to form new habits and to use the phrase, "After I do this, I will do this", when creating a trigger.

We celebrate because we want to tell ourselves, hey, you did a good job. We are telling our brain that we liked what we did just now, it made us feel good, remember this feeling and create a pattern so that we can do this everyday.

#### Question 6: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?

We can a make a habit easier to do by making it obvious, making it attractive, reducing the friction to do it, and make it immediately satisfying.

#### Question 7: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

We can a make a habit harder to do by making it invisible, making it fugly, increasing the friction to do it, and make it unsatisfying to do.

#### Question 8: Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and or response satisfying?

I want to be able to study and learn more.

- Make the cue obvious: I will leave my textbook or laptop with the course open on the dining table (central place, thus point of frequent convergence) and make it a point to sit down and read a page or watch course work as and when I pass through the dining hall.
- Make the habit attractive: I will get snack right after I read or watch making the studying more attractive.
- Make the habit easy: I will not force myself to learn something in one shot. I will allow myself re-read or re-watch something to take the pressure surrounding studying out. I will also restrict myself to consume smaller chunks of knowledge so that I don't get overwhelmed.
- Make the habit satisfying: I get to eat my favourite snacks after (not a healthy habit, I will have to sub it out for something else later on).

#### Question 9: Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue invisible or the process unattractive or hard or the response unsatisfying?

I want to stop scrolling on my phone all the time.

- Make the cue invisible: I would keep the phone out of reach and not at an arm's length away.
- Make the habit unattractive: I would force myself to chew on bitter-gourd (or anything unpleasant. Turns out food is my motivator and demotivator as well. Need to change this).
- Make the habit hard: I would uninstall every social media app and block their sites on my browser and install a minimalist theme to make my phone unattractive.
- Make the habit unsatisfying: If I catch myself unlocking my phone, I would force myself to open the notes app and type "I am a bad human" and force myself to read it out loud. Till I am disgusted by my phone (yes, this is the length I will go to).
